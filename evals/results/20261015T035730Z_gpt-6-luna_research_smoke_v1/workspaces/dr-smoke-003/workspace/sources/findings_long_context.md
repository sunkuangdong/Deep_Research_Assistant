# Findings: Long-Context Model Capabilities and Limitations for Enterprise QA

## Research scope
This file covers empirical evidence about long-context retrieval and position effects, official guidance relevant to long-context prompting, and limits on what can be inferred for enterprise question answering. It does not assess unrelated model capabilities or enterprise deployment controls.

## Key findings
- **Long input capacity does not guarantee uniform use of the input.** Liu et al. evaluate multi-document question answering and key-value retrieval, finding that performance can change substantially with the position of relevant information. Across tested models, performance was often strongest when evidence appeared near the beginning or end and weaker when it appeared in the middle, including for models marketed or designed for long contexts. This is evidence of a position sensitivity in the tested tasks and models, not proof that every current model or enterprise corpus exhibits the same curve.
  Source: [Liu et al., “Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172)
- **Anthropic’s own Claude 2.1 guidance reports both strong retrieval and a specific failure mode.** Anthropic describes a 200K-token-context experiment and says Claude 2.1 could retrieve an in-context sentence across the window, but could be reluctant to answer when a sentence was anomalous or out of place. In its internal needle-style evaluation, adding “Here is the most relevant sentence in the context:” raised the reported score from 27% to 98%; the article also reports 90–95% accuracy on a separate in-context Yahoo/Viaweb example with the revised prompt. These are vendor-reported results for Claude 2.1 and the described setups, not independent, general-purpose enterprise QA benchmarks.
  Source: [Anthropic, “Long context prompting for Claude 2.1”](https://claude.com/resources/articles/claude-2-1-prompting)
- **The evidence is not simply “middle always fails.”** Anthropic reports that, after randomizing essay order, Claude 2.1 answered its Yahoo/Viaweb retrieval question correctly regardless of where the relevant line appeared, without the special prompt. This contrasts with the broader position sensitivity reported by Liu et al. and shows that outcomes can depend on model, task, evidence type, prompt, and whether the evidence is contextually anomalous. It does not invalidate the position-effect finding; it cautions against treating it as a universal deterministic rule.
- **Prompting can change retrieval behavior, but cannot establish factual reliability by itself.** Anthropic’s prompt edit appears to reduce reluctance to surface a relevant sentence in its test. The article frames the original reluctance partly as calibration against unsupported claims. Encouraging extraction may improve recall while also potentially increasing unsupported answers; the cited experiment does not establish the precision/recall tradeoff for enterprise corpora. QA systems should evaluate both correct retrieval and abstention/grounding behavior.
- **What can reasonably be inferred for enterprise QA:** long-context models can process and retrieve information from large inputs, and position, prompt framing, and evidence salience may affect answer quality. Long-context capacity alone is not evidence that all relevant passages will be found or synthesized reliably. Evaluation should use representative enterprise documents, vary evidence position and distractor density, and measure grounded accuracy, omissions, and unsupported claims.
- **What cannot be inferred:** these sources do not establish a universal context-length threshold, a guaranteed accuracy level for current models, performance on a particular company’s documents, or that a single prompt adjustment solves retrieval failures. Liu et al.’s results are task/model-specific and from a 2023 study; Anthropic’s figures are vendor-reported Claude 2.1 experiments. Neither source alone supports a direct ranking of current enterprise models or a claim that long-context QA replaces retrieval-augmented or other evidence-selection workflows.

## Evidence and uncertainty
The two sources provide complementary but non-identical evidence: a peer-reviewed study reports position sensitivity across evaluated models/tasks, while Anthropic’s official article reports strong results in selected Claude 2.1 retrieval setups and a prompt-sensitive reluctance failure mode. The apparent conflict is plausibly explained by differences in task construction, model, prompt, and whether the target sentence is anomalous; the sources do not provide a controlled head-to-head comparison. Generalization to current model versions and real enterprise QA remains uncertain. The evidence supports testing position robustness and grounding on the intended workload, not assuming either universal failure or universal success.
